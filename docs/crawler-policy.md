# Crawler policy, version 1.0.0

The canonical registry is [crawlers.v1.json](../config/crawlers.v1.json), verified against the linked vendor documentation on 2026-11-04. This is scoring and extraction groundwork, not a robots parser or a live access test. Its entries distinguish search crawlers, AI search/retrieval crawlers, user-triggered fetchers, and training-related crawlers. These labels describe the documented role relevant to this policy, not every possible use of a crawl.

## S5 scoring and observations

Evaluate the effective robots policy for the audited product URL and each scored user-agent, using the appropriate origin, group, path, and documented crawler-specific behavior. Googlebot and Bingbot each contribute 0.5 points; OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, and PerplexityBot each contribute one third of a point. Allowed earns the allocation; explicitly disallowed earns zero. Keep thirds as exact fractions during calculation, rounding only for display. The published registry fixes the denominator; do not silently replace bots or redistribute their weights when evidence is missing.

Each scored bot needs an allowed, disallowed, or not-measured observation with its evidence. If any scored result is not measured, the S5 total is incomplete and is represented as null; do not award zero, assume access, or publish a complete total. Examples in the registry include all observations for scored bots; their numerical expectations describe the full two-point S5. `not_measured` also covers unresolved fetch errors or unclear effective rules. A confirmed restriction merits a prominent access warning without a second numerical penalty; ask whether it is intentional before recommending changes. A page that cannot be assessed is unable to assess, without an overall numerical score.

Training-related restrictions, including GPTBot and ClaudeBot, incur no S5 deduction. User-triggered fetchers are also outside the score and should be reported separately. OpenAI says [ChatGPT-User robots rules may not apply](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots); Anthropic says [Claude-User respects its robots controls](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler); Perplexity says [Perplexity-User generally ignores robots rules](https://docs.perplexity.ai/docs/resources/perplexity-crawlers). A rule for one does not establish another's behavior. OpenAI notes its search and training settings are independent, although one crawl may serve both when both are allowed.

## Interpreting evidence

A missing file is not automatically a failure. Google documents treating 4xx responses other than 429 as no robots file, and its handling of 5xx/network errors changes with time and cached rules: it initially stops crawling, then may use cached rules or other documented behavior. The registry's `defer` is only this initial behavior, not a complete error algorithm. See [Google's robots specification](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/robots-txt/robots-txt-spec). Other entries retain unknown missing/error policies where the cited vendor material does not establish them. General protocol defaults are not vendor-confirmed behavior. Unresolved errors and unparseable or inaccessible responses are not permission; preserve uncertainty rather than guessing. A documented opt-out makes the three retrieval bots eligible for this registry; it does not establish their every parsing, status, or caching rule. A future implementation must substantiate each interpretation and leave unsupported cases unmeasured.

Bing documents that a bot-specific group supersedes its generic group and that Bingbot honors Crawl-delay; Crawl-delay is a rate instruction, not Disallow. See [Bing's robots guidance](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/how-to-create-a-robots-txt-file-cb7c31ec) and [Bingbot guidance](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/how-to-report-an-issue-with-bingbot-25c19802). Avoid extrapolating this group behavior to every vendor.

`documented_js` means a vendor documents JavaScript rendering, not that a particular page or resource rendered successfully. Google documents a separate render queue and limitations for blocked resources in its [JavaScript guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics). `unknown` means the cited material does not establish rendering capability; it does not mean the bot cannot render. No fetch-only claim is inferred from a user-agent string.

Robots permission is distinct from actual retrieval, indexing, snippets, login, WAF or challenge barriers, and citation. A fetch using a spoofed user-agent shows only what that test received; it does not prove access for the real crawler. Where relevant, vendors publish IP-verification guidance in their linked sources. Record raw observations and uncertainty separately from policy conclusions. This registry is a dated snapshot: review official documentation and version the registry and dependent expectations when eligibility or behavior changes.
